## Narration (full script)
In 165 AD, Rome was the most powerful civilization the world had ever seen.

Marcus Aurelius, its emperor, was a philosopher — a man who never wanted power and spent his reign trying to be worthy of it.

Roman legions had just crushed the Parthian Empire in the east — the greatest military victory in a generation.

When the soldiers came home, they brought something with them.

A plague swept from the eastern frontier into the heart of the empire.

It moved through cities, military camps, and trade routes across the entire empire.

Five million dead. A third of the Roman army gone.

In the middle of the crisis, Marcus Aurelius wrote — private notes on how to endure.

Those notes became Meditations. Still read today.

Rome survived the plague. But it was never the same.

The Antonine Plague began a slow unraveling that would take three centuries to complete.

Rome fell. The Meditations survived.
